## OCCT source pointer + prose

Branch (prose-laundered): ConvertSurfaceToBezierBasis splits a
B-spline surface at knots to produce Bezier patches. When the input
surface is self-intersecting (e.g. control net folds back on itself
in one parametric direction), the per-patch Bezier conversion
silently produces patches whose Bezier control nets are individually
non-self-intersecting but whose patch boundaries overlap in 3D —
downstream consumers see distinct patches that occupy the same 3D
region.

## Attempts

### Attempt 1 (wave-N) — WEAK_VALID

- **Approach**: B-spline surface with a control-net fold in the
  U-direction, plus a zero-length pcurve edge marking the intended
  fold location for clarity.
- **Refutation**: Adversarial reviewer noted the self-intersection
  is *visual* — the control-net positions imply a fold, but the
  fixture doesn't pre-load any state that demonstrates ConvertSurfaceToBezierBasis
  actually misbehaves on it. The zero-length edge added "for clarity"
  is itself a separate degenerate structure that hints at malformation
  but doesn't trigger the convert-to-Bezier path.

## Why it's hard

The defect is at the Bezier conversion step, which is normally invoked
*after* an upstream surface-quality check would have flagged the
self-intersection. So the fixture needs a self-intersecting surface
that *passes* the upstream check (so the convert step is reached)
while still triggering the defect there. That's a tight target.

## Next directions

- Remove the zero-length edge — it's adding noise, not signal.
- Build the surface so the self-intersection is *parametric*
  (knot-vector arrangement causes patch overlap on conversion) rather
  than 3D-control-net folding. Parametric overlap is the case
  ConvertSurfaceToBezierBasis specifically mishandles.
- Pair with `ShapeUpgrade_ShapeConvertToBezier::Perform` invocation
  in a kernel-test-pair to observe the resulting Bezier patches'
  boundaries.
- Look at upstream surface checks (e.g. `ShapeAnalysis_Surface::IsDegenerated`)
  to verify which self-intersection forms slip through them.
