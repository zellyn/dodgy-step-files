  • gate reached from `IsMergedClosed` `:1255-1257` via `FindCandidates` `:1688-1689`). Closure tolerance being tested: `Geom_BSplineSurface::IsUClosed` (`Geom_BSplineSurface_1.cxx:1216`) compares the two boundary U-isocurves with `Precision::Confusion()`.

Description

One `ADVANCED_FACE` on a `B_SPLINE_SURFACE_WITH_KNOTS`, degree 1 × 1, shaped as a 12-sided prism of radius 1 and height 2: 13 pole columns wrapping the full 360°, where the LAST column is the first column displaced radially by 1e-4 instead of being identical to it. That is 1000× the 1e-7 pole-comparison tolerance, so the surface reports itself NOT closed in U (and it is not closed in V either) while being geometrically closed for every practical purpose. The face's outer bound is the classic seam loop — bottom polyline, the seam line at u=umax reversed, top polyline reversed, the seam line at u=umin reversed — so ONE face carries two distinct, unshared, 1e-4-apart vertical edges that are seam-merge candidates for each other.

Reproducer recipe

`B_SPLINE_SURFACE_WITH_KNOTS` of degree 1 in both directions whose pole grid wraps a full revolution with the last pole column offset from the first by a distance well above `Precision::Confusion()` but far below the model scale (here 1e-4 on a radius-1 prism); one `ADVANCED_FACE` on it bounded by bottom/top polyline `B_SPLINE_CURVE_WITH_KNOTS` edges and the two straight seam-line edges at u=umin and u=umax; sew at tolerance 1e-2.

Expected kernel behavior

the two same-face seam-line edges are recognized as a genuine seam pair and merged, even though the surface exposes no closure flag saying it wraps: the closure test falls back on sampling isoparametric curves and comparing each one's endpoint-to-endpoint distance against its endpoint-to-midpoint distance, which detects the wrap; the 2D-overlap conditions then approve the merge.

Notes

Why we believe this test is valid — live-verified against this fixture's own bytes (this worktree's `validation/.venv`, OCP/OCCT 7.8.1). Default read → shape_null=False, 1 face, 4 edges; `BRep_Tool::Surface(face)` is a `Geom_BSplineSurface` whose `IsUClosed()`, `IsVClosed()`, `IsUPeriodic()` and `IsVPeriodic()` all return **False** (asserted live, not assumed), and all four edges carry pcurves on it. Feeding the read shape to `BRepBuilderAPI_Sewing(1e-2)` and calling `Perform()` MERGES the two seam-line edges: `NbContigousEdges()==1`, `NbMultipleEdges()==0`, 4 unique edges → 3, and `ContigousEdgeCouple(1)` names exactly the two sections `[(1,0,2),(1,0,0)]` and `[(1.0001,0,2),(1.0001,0,0)]` — the u=umin and u=umax seam lines, averaged to x=1.00005. Both belong to the SAME face, so that merge is gated: `FindCandidates` computes `isOK = !Faces1.Contains(Face2)` and, when false, defers to `IsMergedClosed`, whose first act is `if (!isUClosed && !isVClosed) return Standard_False` with both flags coming from `IsUClosedSurface`/`IsVClosedSurface`. Since the surface itself reports neither U- nor V-closed, the merge can only have been approved through the isoparametric-curve fallback inside those two methods. Two controls from the same generator confirm the sensitivity: with the pole gap set to 0 (columns exactly coincident) OCCT hands back a U-PERIODIC surface (`IsUClosed()==True`), so the fallback is bypassed entirely — which is precisely why the 1e-4 gap has to be there; with the wrap reduced 360°→180° (a genuinely open patch, seam lines 2 apart) `NbContigousEdges()==0` and nothing merges. Sewing is not on the default STEP read path, so the merge itself requires the sewing scaffold to observe; the closure-flag facts are observable directly from the default read. Synonyms: "surface closed within tolerance but not flagged closed", "seam merge without periodic flag STEP", "IsClosedByIsos isoparametric closure fallback", "bspline pole gap seam sewing", "effectively closed surface seam pair merge".
