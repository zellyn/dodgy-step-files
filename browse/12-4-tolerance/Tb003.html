Tb003 — Vertex-edge-face tolerance hierarchy holds in mm but inverts in m

§12.4 (sub-class: hierarchy × unit-scaling)

P1 — silent data loss

Sources

  • synthesized time-bomb.

Description

A face declares uncertainty 1e-4 mm, an edge declares 1e-5 mm, a vertex declares 1e-6 mm — the OCCT-style invariant `tol(V) <= tol(E) <= tol(F)` holds. The receiver imports the file and internally scales coordinates from mm to m (factor 1e-3). The numeric tolerance values, if naively divided by 1000, become 1e-7, 1e-8, 1e-9 — but if the receiver also applies a working-precision floor (e.g. `max(tol, 1e-9)`), the floor flattens the small ones and inverts the hierarchy. Or the receiver may not scale tolerances at all, leaving them in source units while geometry is in target units. Same input, two different scaling policies, two different post-import hierarchies.

Reproducer recipe

A `MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP` with three `UNCERTAINTY_MEASURE_WITH_UNIT` records (1e-6, 1e-5, 1e-4) labelled vertex/edge/face. The file's `LENGTH_UNIT` is `.MILLI.METRE.`. Receiver may rescale to `.METRE.`.

Expected kernel behavior

produce a consistent hierarchy regardless of internal unit scaling; either rescale tolerances atomically with coordinates, or document a single canonical working-unit and reject input that cannot survive the conversion.
