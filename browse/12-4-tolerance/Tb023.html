Tb023 — Coordinates near 1e15 where float64 ULP exceeds declared tolerance

§12.4 (sub-class: far-from-origin precision)

deduced — exporters from GIS-aware CAD or reality-capture pipelines that store world coordinates without local recentering

Description

A producer emits coordinates near 1e15 — for example a model whose origin sits at a real-world UTM-style position. At magnitudes near 1e15, the float64 unit-in-the-last-place is ~0.125 (the spacing between adjacent representable doubles is 2^(53-50) ≈ 2^(-2) at exponent 2^50 ≈ 1.1e15). A `LENGTH_UNIT` of `MM` plus a declared `UNCERTAINTY` of `0.001 mm` sets a kernel's working tolerance below float64's representable spacing at the model's coordinates. Vertex equality, edge connectivity, sliver-detection — every tolerance comparison is dominated by floating-point noise rather than geometric meaning. Two vertices that should be coincident at sub-tolerance distance can differ by 0.125 mm because they round to adjacent representable doubles. Bug-reporter language: "STEP coordinates near 1e15", "GIS-style absolute coords", "model far from origin loses precision", "vertices wobble at huge coords".

Reproducer recipe

a `CARTESIAN_POINT` with coordinates `(1.234567890123e15, 2.345678901234e15, 3.456789012345e15)` and a sibling point at `(1.234567890124e15, 2.345678901234e15, 3.456789012345e15)` — the two points differ by 1e3 in the original-units description but, after rounding to float64, occupy the same or adjacent representable doubles. Declared `UNCERTAINTY_MEASURE_WITH_UNIT(LENGTH_MEASURE(0.001), MILLIMETRE)`.

Expected kernel behavior

surface the precision gap when the working tolerance is below the local ULP at the model's coordinates; recommend recentering to a local frame near the origin; reject as numerically meaningless if the user requests strict tolerance handling at far-from-origin scale.
