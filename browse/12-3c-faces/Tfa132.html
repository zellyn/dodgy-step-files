Description

B-spline surface with a genuinely duplicated control-point column (real C0 fold, not a placeholder) causes the reader to drop the face entirely: the OPEN_SHELL survives as an empty container (0 faces, 0 wires, 0 edges); twisted-face checking is never reached

Reproducer recipe

Expected kernel behavior

Accept: a face whose surface control-point mesh contains a genuine C0 fold (here two adjacent control-point columns sharing identical coordinates at every row) is still valid encoded geometry, so the reader must build and keep the face — do not silently drop it from the shell and leave an empty container behind; if a healing pass is explicitly requested to address the C0 discontinuity, it should reparametrize or split the surface at the fold rather than deleting the whole face.

Notes

Corrected 2026-07-17 (truth-in-labeling audit): retitled/redescribed — original claimed twisted-face checking misclassifies the surface at its C0 discontinuity, while separately (and inconsistently) describing the fixture as a geometry-less placeholder; live-oracle shows the fixture DOES carry a genuine duplicated-control-point B-spline surface, and the real, verified effect is that the face is silently dropped from the shell entirely during translation (0 faces survive in an otherwise non-null shell), not a twist misclassification. Part of the ~127-entry face-repair/small-face-check misnomer family — see `BACKLOG.md` Q14. Synonyms: "duplicated B-spline control-point column drops face from shell", "C0-fold surface face silently dropped, shell survives empty".
