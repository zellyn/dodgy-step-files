Ps007 — Assembly child placed at identity instead of intended offset

§12.3a shells / orientation (filed here per Ps prefix; conceptually §12.6 assembly)

P1 — silent data loss

Sources

  • deduced from ITEM_DEFINED_TRANSFORMATION semantics
  • bug-report patterns "component at origin", "assembly children all stacked at 0,0,0", "NAUO transform lost", "child position not applied"

Description

A NEXT_ASSEMBLY_USAGE_OCCURRENCE wires the right parent / child product pair, but the bound REPRESENTATION_RELATIONSHIP_WITH_TRANSFORMATION's ITEM_DEFINED_TRANSFORMATION uses the same AXIS2_PLACEMENT_3D for its source and target; yielding the identity transform. The producer's intent was a non-identity offset (e.g., a bolt at (50,50,0) in the assembly's frame). Topology and references all validate; kernel reports one part instance attached to the assembly. Tier-3 introspection (resolved transform = source^-1 · target compared to identity, plus a "did-the-instance-actually-move" check) reveals the placement is ineffectual.

Reproducer recipe

Standard PRODUCT chain with one assembly PRODUCT and one child PRODUCT. NAUO with id='1' linking them. The ITEM_DEFINED_TRANSFORMATION uses AXIS2_PLACEMENT_3D #701 for both transform_item_1 and transform_item_2 (or two distinct placements with identical location and axes).

Expected kernel behavior

Warn and accept: detect identity transforms in assembly-instance edges (or, more strongly, surface the resolved transform of every NAUO so downstream code can confirm it matches the producer's intent); emit a diagnostic noting the identity-transform fact.

Notes

Tier-3 metric: resolved-transform matrix per NAUO; flag any whose Frobenius distance to identity is below working precision. Synonyms: "component at origin", "assembly children all stacked at 0,0,0", "NAUO transform lost", "child position not applied", "every part shows up at the same point".

Expected validation

occt=shape(1)/shape(1) gmsh=shape(27) ifc=schema_n/a

Tier-3 assertions

  • n_edges_total >= 24
  • face[0].surface_type == "plane"
  • face[5].surface_type == "plane"
