Ad110 — `.stpz` gzip wrapper expands ratio 1:10000 into multi-gigabyte body

Sender

deduced — adversarial input that exercises the .stpz gzip-wrapper decompression path with a highly-compressible payload

Description

The `.stpz` extension denotes a gzip-wrapped Part-21 file. Gzip compresses long runs of identical bytes at ratios approaching 1:1024 per DEFLATE block, and a multi-block stream can chain to an aggregate ratio of 1:10000 or more. An attacker ships a .stpz file of ~3 KB whose decompressed body is 30+ MB of repeated `;` or whitespace; with multiple stacked gzip members or a single very-redundant payload, the decompressed size reaches gigabytes. A receiver that calls a streaming gunzip into a single buffer exhausts RAM. Distinct from Ad027 / Pf030 (instance-count bombs at the parser layer): the bomb is at the gzip layer and triggers before any Part-21 token is seen. Bug-reporter language: "stpz decompression bomb", "gzip ratio explodes", "Part-21 reader OOMs on tiny .stpz".

Reproducer recipe

a `.stpz` file consisting of a gzip stream whose payload is e.g. 4 GB of repeated `' '` bytes plus a minimal Part-21 envelope; the compressed file is ~4 KB. The fixture in this catalog is the embedded Part-21 body for cataloguing only; the bomb itself lives in the gzip wrapper.

Expected kernel behavior

cap the per-stream decompressed-byte budget; abort decompression when the budget is exceeded with a precise diagnostic; treat the gzip layer as adversarial. Stream the decompressed bytes through the Part-21 tokeniser rather than buffering the whole expansion.

Notes

Distinct from Ad027 (instance-count bomb at parser layer) and Ad105 (archive header lies about size — Ad105 is detectable from the header alone, this entry's gzip stream actually produces the bytes when decoded). **See also**: Ad027, Ad105, Ad111, Ad112, Pf030. Provenance tier: cross-file-state — bytes alone (a Part-21 body) cannot demonstrate this defect; the bomb is in the gzip wrapper.
